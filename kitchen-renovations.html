What we handle in a kitchen renovation

Kitchen renovations usually bring in floor tiling and splashback tiling as the tiling side of a wider project. Ceramic and mosaic tiles are a common choice for splashbacks — they're straightforward to keep clean and sit well behind a stove or sink — while porcelain or natural stone tiles are the more usual pick for kitchen floors.

Worth noting: cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and appliance installation are specialist trades outside our tiling and surface-preparation work. What's included in the wider renovation depends on the agreed scope of the job.

Splashback and floor tiling

Splashback tiling is one of the more visible parts of a kitchen renovation, so the set-out around powerpoints, the stove and the sink gets worked out before installation starts. Tile cuts and grout lines are considered as part of the layout rather than left to the end. Floor tiling across a kitchen needs a properly prepared and suitable substrate, particularly where it ties in with an adjoining living or dining area at the same floor height.

Preparing surfaces for kitchen tiling

The surface underneath the tiles matters just as much as the tiles themselves. Before installation, we check that the substrate is suitable, deal with preparation requirements and make sure the finished tile surface will work with surrounding floors, walls and cabinetry.

Fitting in with the rest of the renovation

Where a kitchen renovation involves several trades, the tiling and surface-preparation stages are planned around the wider renovation so the tiled areas are ready at the right point — not too early, and not holding up cabinetry or benchtop installation.
