Tile insert strip drain

Project 04 · Hawthorn

Back to Projects

/The challenge

A Drain That Disappears Into the Floor

This wet-area floor in Hawthorn was finished with large-format tiles and a tile insert strip drain — a linear drain with a channel that takes a strip of the floor tile, so the drain sits almost flush and reads as part of the floor.

A tile insert drain only looks right if everything lines up. The tile layout has to be set out around the drain, the floor has to fall toward it, and the strip of tile in the channel has to match the tiles on either side — otherwise the drain stands out instead of disappearing.

/Common questions

Questions about strip drains and wet-area floors

It’s a linear floor drain with a recessed channel on top that takes a strip of the same tile as the floor. Once it’s fitted, only thin slots are visible, so the drain blends into the tiling.

A strip drain runs in a straight line, so the floor only needs to fall in one direction toward it. That suits large-format tiles, which are difficult to cut and slope in the several directions a centre floor waste needs.

Yes — it’s one of the most common reasons to choose one. The single fall toward a linear drain lets large tiles sit with fewer cuts across the floor.

In wet areas, preparation may involve screeding to establish the required levels and falls, followed by waterproofing before tiling.
